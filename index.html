Should we launch a take-back and resale programme?

Mid-market fashion brand: 200,000 active customers, average price €90, 60% gross margin on new products. 10,000 simulated years. Generated 18 Oct 2026.

Recommendation

Launch a small pilot with a partner model. It is close to break-even, not a sure win. The partner route makes money in 60% of simulated years (median €15k), against 3% for running it in-house (median -€200k).

At this size, running it in-house does not break even even if 30% of customers took part: the fixed costs of a team, software and warehouse space need a much bigger brand.

The biggest unknown is resale price, % of original price. It moves profit more than any other assumption, so it is the first thing to test, for example with a three-month pilot in a few stores.

Profit is not the only return: the programme brings customers back to the brand through vouchers and keeps clothes in use. In the most likely case it avoids about 19 tonnes of CO2e a year.

The partner model assumes the partner handles inspection, cleaning, listing and selling in exchange for a share of resale revenue and a yearly fee; the brand still pays return shipping and vouchers. Vouchers are costed at face value; the brand earns its gross margin only on the share of voucher purchases that would not have happened otherwise.
